**The question.** AI data centers cool their servers with water, and they use more of it on hot days. Wetlands are known to cool the land around them. If that cooling reaches nearby facilities, protecting wetlands could save water. Nobody had measured this for the world's biggest data center cluster.

**What I did.** I compared satellite surface temperature at Occoquan Bay wetland with the Ashburn "Data Center Alley" core over six years, then modelled one facility's monthly groundwater withdrawals against air temperature and its 2023 cooling retrofit.

**What I found, and how sure I am.** The wetland runs about 3.3 °C cooler in summer, and the facility withdraws more water in hotter months. But the pilot covers one wetland, one urban area and one facility, and the temperature effect weakens once month-of-year effects are controlled. The results are suggestive, not final; the next phase scales up to hundreds of wetlands and every reporting facility in the region.
